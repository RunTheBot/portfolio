My high school shop had a 25-year-old Larken 24/24 CNC router sitting dormant because its uses bespoke software. The frame, lead screws, and stepper motors were in perfect condition, but the electronics were completely obsolete. I designed AstralCNC, a custom open-hardware controller PCB based on the Raspberry Pi RP2040/RP2350 to run the open sourced GRBL-HAL firmware. 

### Key Engineering Decisions

- **Hardware-Level E-Stop Latch:** If a microcontroller ever hung, software-based safety stops could fail. I designed a physical hardware latching circuit to handle the emergency stop button directly without relying on MCU firmware.
- **5V I/O:** Driven by 74HC244 line to output 5V logic pulses to external stepper drivers as the machine operated on 5v while the controller was on 3.3v.
- **Integrated Power Delivery:** Onboard AP63357 buck converter steps down the machine's 24V/36V main power supply to clean 5V and 3.3V rails.

### Real-World Machine Specs

- **Microcontroller:** RP2040 / RP2350 running grblHAL with USB and serial control.
- **Axis Support:** Up to 8 step/direction channels with configurable microstepping and hardware pulse rates up to 200 kHz per axis.

### Results

The 25-year-old router is now fully operational, cutting polycarbonate panels for our Vex Robotics team and course work. Schematics, gerbers, and BOM are fully open-sourced on GitHub.
